A short-video opening should help viewers understand what they are about to see. A dramatic claim is only useful if the rest of the video can support it. There is no wording formula that guarantees reach or retention.

## Start with the task or result

For a product demo, show the task on screen and name it: “Paste a release note, then turn it into a short update.” For a tutorial, show the actual result before explaining the steps. For an opinion, state the specific situation you are discussing.

Avoid invented losses, health risks, income figures, and urgency. Use a number only when you have evidence and can explain its context.

## Write several truthful alternatives

Try a question the video answers, a result the video demonstrates, and a concise statement of the problem. Keep each version understandable without background context. These are drafting options, not measured winners.

Read the opening aloud and check it against the first shot. Remove setup that repeats the title, and avoid promising a result the video does not deliver.

## Evaluate your own results

Compare alternative openings using your own publishing data when available. Record the audience, topic, format, and observation period so that a difference in views is not automatically attributed to the wording. Treat a small test as a limited observation, not a universal rule.
